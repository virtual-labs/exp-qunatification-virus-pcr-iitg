### Procedure

**Step 1: Sample Collection and Handling**
Biological samples including blood, serum, swabs, or cell culture supernatant, suspected of viral presence are collected aseptically. The samples are properly labelled and stored at -20℃ to prevent degradation of viral nucleic acids.

**Step 2: Extraction of Viral Nucleic Acid**
Viral nucleic acid is isolated from the sample using a suitable extraction method such as phenol-chloroform method or a commercial extraction kit.
* For DNA viruses, viral DNA is directly extracted.
* For RNA viruses, viral RNA is isolated carefully to avoid RNase contamination.
* The extracted nucleic acid is quantified and stored at low temperature until further use.

**Step 3: Reverse Transcription (For RNA Viruses Only)**
For RNA viruses, reverse transcription is performed before amplification.
* The extracted RNA is mixed with reverse transcriptase, primers, nucleotides, and buffer.
* The reaction is incubated at specific temperatures to synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA).
* The synthesized cDNA then serves as the template for Real-Time PCR amplification.

**Step 4: Preparation of qPCR Reaction Mixture**
In order to achieve uniformity across all the reactions, a master mix is prepared that typically contains:
* Template DNA or cDNA
* Forward and reverse primers specific to the viral target
* Fluorescent dye or probe
* DNA polymerase with reaction buffer
* dNTPs
* Nuclease-free water

*Care is taken to avoid bubble formation and cross-contamination during preparation.*

**Step 5: Preparation of Standards and Controls**
A standard curve is generated using serial dilutions of viral nucleic acid with known concentrations. Additionally:
* A negative control (no template control) is included to check for contamination.
* A positive control ensures the reaction is functioning properly.
* These controls are necessary for accurate quantification and validation of results.

**Step 6: Loading of Reaction Tubes or Plate**
The prepared reaction mixtures are loaded into PCR tubes or wells of a qPCR plate, sealed properly to prevent evaporation, and then placed into the Real-Time PCR instrument.

**Step 7: Programming the Real-Time PCR Instrument**
The thermal cycling conditions are set according to the assay requirements, typically including:
* Initial denaturation for the enzyme activation and DNA melting
* Repeated cycles of denaturation, annealing, and extension
* Fluorescence data is recorded at the appropriate step during each cycle.

**Step 8: Amplification and Real-Time Detection**
During amplification, the fluorescent signal increases proportionally with the quantity of amplified viral nucleic acid. The instrument continuously monitors fluorescence and displays amplification curves in real time.

**Step 9: Data Analysis and Standard Curve Generation**
The Ct values obtained from standards are plotted against their known concentrations to generate a standard curve. The viral load is calculated by comparing the Ct values of unknown samples with this curve.

**Step 10: Interpretation and Reporting of Results**
The viral concentration in the sample is quantified and expressed as copies per unit volume. Lower Ct values indicate higher viral load, while higher Ct values indicate lower viral concentration. Results are documented and interpreted in the context of experimental controls.
