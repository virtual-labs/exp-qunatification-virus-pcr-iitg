### Principle

Real-Time PCR is a sensitive technique used to amplify and detect a specific nucleic acid sequence simultaneously. Unlike conventional PCR, it enables the detection of the product at every amplification cycle via fluorescent signals.

In viral quantification, the fluorescence intensity is directly proportional to the amount of viral DNA or RNA present in the sample. Viral load is determined by comparing the fluorescence values of unknown samples to a standard curve generated from known concentrations.

### Theory

Viruses possess either DNA or RNA as their genetic material. In the case of RNA viruses, the RNA is first converted to complementary DNA (cDNA) using the enzyme reverse transcriptase, which is then used as a template for PCR amplification.

In Real-Time PCR, specific primers bind to the target viral sequence, and a fluorescent dye or probe is added to the reaction. During amplification, the fluorescence increases and is detected in real time by the instrument.

The cycle threshold (Ct) value indicates the cycle number at which the fluorescence signal exceeds a predefined threshold. Higher Ct values suggest lower viral loads whereas lower Ct values imply higher viral concentrations. Viral quantification is achieved by plotting Ct values against known standards to create a standard curve.
